# Craft: what makes any reel work

The look is the user's (`My Style/`). These rules are about reels in general, whatever the style.

## The frame

- 1080x1920, 30 fps. Keep words and key marks between y=240 and y=1560: Instagram and TikTok cover the rest with buttons and captions.
- Two grounds per style, `main` and `alt`, plus `photo`. Alternate them so each cut is felt.
- The hook is the strongest frame. It must be readable in the first half second: big words, strong contrast.

## Timing

- Every scene starts on its line's first word, and marks and words arrive on the word they belong to (`start_of(line, word)`), not at random times.
- Every scene gets at least 1.2 seconds on screen.
- One new thing about every half second, the first one on the cut, so no scene opens empty.
- Transitions (`"move"` lines) where the topic shifts, 2 to 4 in a 25-second reel. Plain cuts everywhere else. Too many moves feel like a template.
- One full-screen accent moment (`takeover`) at most, on the biggest turn.

## What makes a scene work

- **The picture shows the line.** "Drier than most deserts" means a desert, or a drawn sun; not a random airplane. Simple beats clever. Ask of every scene: would someone with the sound off get it?
- **One thing to look at.** A busy photo gets at most one small mark. A drawn scene gets one big word or number and one drawing or mark.
- **Big words carry one idea, not the caption.** Two or three words per scene ("DRIER", "IS SMELL"), never a repeat of the whole spoken line.
- **Drawn scenes get a drawing.** Words alone feel like a slideshow: add a doodle or a mark that shows the idea.
- **Numbers are shown, not only said.** A number in the script becomes a big number on screen, ideally counting or crossed out.
- **Photos open wide.** Start on the whole picture, then push in. A tight crop at the start leaves people guessing.

## Avoid, in any style

- Pictures used as decoration that don't show the line.
- Icons and labels piled on a busy photo.
- Time stickers ("in 30 seconds") and claims nobody can check.
- Music in the render (people add a song in the app; most music isn't free to use).
- Copying another creator's logo, mascot, watermark or catchphrases.
