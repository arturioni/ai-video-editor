# Writing the script

The script is the reel. A good one survives with the sound off and the pictures removed.

Write in the user's voice: read `My Style/voice.md` first (who it's for, how they talk, words to use or avoid, how they end reels). Where it says nothing, use the defaults below.

## Shape

- **Length:** 60 to 95 words, about 18 to 28 seconds at about 3.3 words a second. Go up to 135 words only if the user asks for a longer reel. Shorter and sharper always wins: never add a line to fill time.
- **Hook (first line):** under 8 words, and it has to make someone stop scrolling. Good hooks contradict what people believe ("It's not the food. It's the plane."), promise a payoff ("Here's why you can't taste anything on a plane."), or open a loop ("Nobody tells you this about airplane food."). No greetings, no "In this video".
- **Middle:** one idea per sentence, in the order that makes the payoff land. Each sentence gets its own picture, so write lines you can picture.
- **Turn:** most good reels have one moment where it flips ("But here's the weird part."). That line can get the reel's one takeover.
- **Last line:** tells people what to do, in a few words: "Follow for more things nobody tells you." or "Comment PLANE and I'll send you the list." or "Save this for your next flight." Use the ending in their `voice.md`, or their keyword if they gave one.

## Words

- Plain spoken English, the way a friend explains something. Read it aloud in your head: if it sounds written, rewrite it.
- No jargon or buzzwords: no "leverage", "unlock", "game-changer", "synergy", unless their audience really talks that way.
- No time claims that can't be true for everyone ("in 30 seconds", "in 5 minutes"). Say it's easy instead.
- Contractions are good (it's, you're, that's). Short words beat long ones.
- Numbers as people say them: "about a third" reads and sounds better than "approximately 33%".
- Write words exactly as they should appear in the captions: the voice reads the script, the captions show it.

## Truth

- Only claims you're confident are true. If a fact is shaky, cut it or soften it ("one reason", "about").
- Don't invent studies, numbers or quotes. If the user's line includes a claim you doubt, say so before using it.
- For a topic that needs sources (health, money, law), keep it to well-known facts and suggest the user double-checks.

## Format of script.txt

Plain text, just the words to be read: no titles, no stage directions, no emoji, no hashtags. Sentences end with . ? or ! so the voice pauses in the right places.

## Example (72 words, about 22 seconds)

> You can't tickle yourself. Here's why. Your brain plans every move your hand makes. So before your fingers even touch your skin, it already knows exactly what's coming. No surprise, no tickle. That's also why a tickle from someone else feels so much stronger. Your brain can't predict it. Scientists even built a robot arm with a tiny delay, and people could almost tickle themselves. Follow for more weird things about your body.

The airplane-food reel in `kit/examples/airplane-food/script.txt` is a second example.
