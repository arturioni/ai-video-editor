# How the text looks

These are the kit's starting choices for edits of filmed footage. Captions use the body font from `My Style/` when the user has set one up (and capitals if their style uses them). For anything else ("captions higher", "bigger hook card"), tell Claude what to change and ask it to update this file, so every later edit follows it.

## Feel

- Easy and natural on the surface, well edited underneath. Never like an ad.
- Text should read like a thoughtful editor's note, not a template.

## Captions

- Inter Medium (weight 500), or the style's body font, 44 px on a 1080x1920 frame.
- White with a soft dark shadow. No box, no outline, no colour.
- Sentence case with the punctuation kept, as the person said it.
- One short clause at a time, about three words. A long clause is split in two. Two lines at most, balanced.
- They sit in the lower third, around 1340 px from the top: under the face, above Instagram's own caption and buttons. If the person's face or hands are there, move them to the calmest empty space instead. Never on the face.
- Each caption appears when its first word is said and leaves when the next one starts.

## Cards

- The same Inter family, a little heavier (600), white with a soft shadow.
- Hook card: larger (about 84 px), in the top third, on screen for 1.5 to 2.5 seconds.
- Point cards: smaller (about 68 px), placed where the person isn't.
- Closing card: `Comment WORD`, with only the word in capitals.
- Gentle fade in and out. Nothing bounces, spins or flashes.

## Sound

- Loudness -14 LUFS, peaks no higher than -1.5 dBTP. That is what Instagram, TikTok and YouTube Shorts play at.
- Pauses shortened to a quarter of a second, breaths kept.

## Avoid

- One-word-at-a-time flashing captions, coloured highlight words, emoji rows.
- Sound effects on every card, a zoom on every cut.
- Neon colours, gradients, logos, progress bars.
- Stock music, unless asked.
